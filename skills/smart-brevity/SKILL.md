# Smart Brevity

Smart Brevity is a writing method built on one observation: readers skim. They give you seconds, not minutes. The method's answer is to put the most important thing first, say it in muscular plain words, tell readers why they should care, and stop.

The goal is **short, not shallow**. You are not dumbing anything down or cutting substance — you are cutting the packaging around the substance. A good Smart Brevity rewrite is typically ~40% shorter than the original and loses nothing the reader needed.

Write like you'd talk to a smart, busy friend at a bar: direct, human, specific. If you wouldn't say the word out loud, don't write it.

## The core structure

Almost every Smart Brevity piece is built from four parts, in this order:

1. **A muscular tease** — the headline or subject line. Six words or fewer if you can. Provocative and specific enough to make a skimmer stop. Not clever wordplay — clarity beats cleverness.
2. **One strong first sentence (the lede)** — the single most important, most memorable thing. If the reader reads nothing else, this is what they keep. Make it news to them: "tell me something I don't know." One sentence, direct and declarative.
3. **Context, signposted with a bolded axiom** — usually **Why it matters:** followed by 1–2 sentences explaining the stakes for *this* reader. This is where you earn their next 30 seconds.
4. **The choice to go deeper** — bullets, short paragraphs, or a **Go deeper:** link with the supporting detail. The reader opts in; you never force them through it to find the point.

Everything after the lede is optional to the reader. Structure the piece so stopping at any point still leaves them informed.

## Axioms

Axioms are bolded signpost phrases that start a short section (1–2 direct sentences or a tight bullet list). They let a skimmer navigate by landmark. The standard set:

**Why it matters:** · **The big picture:** · **What's next:** · **The bottom line:** · **By the numbers:** · **Between the lines:** · **The backstory:** · **Catch up quick:** · **Zoom in:** / **Zoom out:** · **What we're watching:** · **Go deeper:** · **What to expect:** · **The other side:**

Rules of thumb:

- "Why it matters" is the workhorse — use it in nearly every piece. Pick 1–3 others at most; a piece that's all axioms is as unreadable as a wall of text.
- The text after an axiom must deliver what the axiom promises. **By the numbers:** gets numbers. **The other side:** gets the opposing view.
- Axioms end with a colon and are bolded; the sentence after them is not.

## Sentence and word rules

- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence. If a sentence has a comma-stacked chain of clauses, split it.
- **Short paragraphs.** 1–2 sentences. Never bury the point in paragraph three.
- **Strong, plain words.** Prefer the one-syllable word: "use" not "utilize," "help" not "facilitate," "start" not "commence," "buy" not "purchase."
- **Active voice, active verbs.** "The board killed the deal," not "the deal was not approved by the board."
- **Kill hedges.** "Could," "may," "might," "somewhat," "arguably" are fog. Either you know it or you cut it. (Keep a hedge only when uncertainty *is* the news.)
- **Kill throat-clearing.** Delete openers like "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "As you may know," "It's worth noting that." Start with the point.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "Revenue fell 22%" beats "revenue fell sharply."
- **One big idea per piece.** If you have two big ideas, write two pieces (or lead with one and bullet the other).

## Formatting

- **Bullets** for any list of 2+ parallel facts, reasons, or steps. Each bullet is one tight sentence or fragment — not a paragraph wearing a bullet.
- **Bold** the axioms, plus the handful of key numbers or phrases a skimmer must catch. Bolding everything bolds nothing — a few per piece.
- **White space is a feature.** Dense blocks signal "skip me."
- Emojis: only where the medium already uses them (Slack, newsletters), sparingly, to convey tone or aid scanning — never as decoration in formal docs.

## Adapt the dose to the medium

Smart Brevity is principles first, format second. The full axiom treatment fits some media; others just get the discipline. Match the reader's context:

| Medium | What to apply |
|---|---|
| Newsletter / announcement / status update | Full structure: tease, lede, **Why it matters:**, bullets, **Go deeper:**. Keep each item under ~200 words. |
| Email | 6-word subject that states the news or the ask. First sentence = the ask or the news. One axiom at most. Bullets for details. If the email needs a decision, say exactly what you need and by when. |
| Memo / report / doc | Open with a 2–3 sentence BLUF (lede + why it matters), then axiom-led sections. Long detail lives at the end or in an appendix — the opt-in principle at document scale. |
| Slack / chat message | No headline, usually no axioms. Just the discipline: point first, short sentences, bullets if listing, bold the ask. |
| Meeting agenda | One-sentence objective, max three bullets, end with owners and next steps. |
| Presentation | One message per slide, ≤12 slides, each point absorbable in ~3 seconds. |

When rewriting someone's existing draft, keep their facts, voice, and any required content — Smart Brevity changes the delivery, not the substance or the author's personality.

## Example

**Before** (an internal update, 118 words):

> Hi everyone, I hope you all had a great weekend. I wanted to give you a quick update on where things stand with the Meridian project. As you may be aware, we have been working closely with the vendor over the past several weeks to try to resolve the integration issues that were identified during testing. Unfortunately, despite everyone's best efforts, it appears that we may not be able to hit the September 15 launch date that we had previously communicated to stakeholders. We are currently thinking that a more realistic target would likely be sometime in early October. We will keep you posted as we learn more and appreciate your patience.

**After** (Smart Brevity, 55 words):

> **Meridian launch slips to October**
>
> Meridian will miss its Sept. 15 launch — new target: the week of Oct. 6.
>
> **Why it matters:** Stakeholders were promised September. We need to reset expectations this week, before they hear it secondhand.
>
> - **The cause:** Vendor integration bugs found in testing.
> - **What's next:** Fix ETA Friday; revised timeline to stakeholders Monday.

Note what happened: the news moved from sentence four to the headline, hedges ("may not," "likely," "sometime") became a date, and the reader knows the stakes and the plan in one glance.

## Self-check before delivering

Run the draft through this list — fix anything that fails:

1. Does the first sentence carry the single most important thing?
2. Would the reader know why they should care within the first 3 lines?
3. Is the headline/subject ≤ ~6 words and specific?
4. Can a reader stop at any point and still have the essentials?
5. Did you cut every hedge, throat-clear, and multi-syllable word with a shorter twin?
6. Is anything bolded that a skimmer doesn't need? Is anything a skimmer needs not bolded?
7. Is it roughly 40% shorter than a first-draft version would be — without losing a fact the reader needed?
